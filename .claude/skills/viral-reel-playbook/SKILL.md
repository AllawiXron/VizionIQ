# Viral reel playbook

Ali sent five reels (27 Oct 2026) and two more on 29 Oct (references 6 and 7) and asked that every session learns from them and applies them. Four are by a
clothing-brand owner who breaks down his own viral reels to camera; one is a sound-design tip. The per-reel notes,
with timings, are in `references.md` next to this file. This page is the rules.

Use it together with CLAUDE.md, not instead of it: the honesty, -noAI, sound-pack, font and size rules there still
win. Where something here touches one of them, the note says how to fit both.

## 1. The idea: every reel is a question and its answer

- **Every great hook creates a question in the viewer's head.** If the viewer is not asking anything, they have no
  reason to stay for an answer. The question is the hook; the answer is the payoff.
- The question is usually raised by **what is on screen**, not by a written question: a man mid-jump (where will he
  land?), a pink wall with an axe (what is he painting?), "I'm not leaving this room until I make $100,000" (will he?),
  a red outline on a path (can I pause in time?). Only sometimes is it said outright ("Can he ace a final with one hour
  of studying?").
- **Structure: hook > build-up > payoff.** The hook asks; the build-up tells a story that keeps the question open (that
  is the watch time); the payoff answers it and gives a feeling. Feeling is what makes people like, comment, share
  and rewatch.
- Before writing any reel script, write down in one line: *what question will the viewer have in the first second, and
  at which second is it answered?* If there is no answer to that, the idea is not ready. Put this line at the top of
  every pitch to Ali.
- Fit with -noAI section 3: that rule bans the *written* pattern "question? answer." in one breath («الحل؟ ..»). Here
  the answer comes at the end of the reel, after the build-up, and usually the question is not written at all. Never
  answer it in the next line.

## 2. Watch time beats views: make them rewatch

- The best reel in the set is two seconds long and got an average watch time far longer than itself, so Instagram
  pushed it to 13.5M views. People replayed it because it was a **challenge**: "Only 1% can pause at the right time",
  a red outline on the path, the walker passing through it, and text flashing by too fast to read ("SOOOO CLOSE, 1
  frame off", "You did it!").
- Teasing text that flashes past makes people pause and replay to read it. Use it on purpose.
- Length is not the goal; the ratio of watch time to length is. Short loops that are replayed win.

## 3. Make the viral reel sell: hide the offer inside the game

- The same 2-second challenge sold out a clothing drop: the frame you win on says "New sweatsuit, live now, DM us a
  screenshot for a discount". About 29,000 people sent DMs with the screenshot. A viral format plus one extra layer
  turns attention into sales, and the DMs themselves are the leads.
- Pattern to reuse: a viral mechanic (pause challenge, spot the difference, find the hidden thing) + a reward only
  the people who play find (a code, a free trial, a free design check) + "send us the screenshot in DM". This plugs
  straight into Ali's DM selling (CLAUDE.md, Selling) and his Messages ads.
- Honesty: the reward must be real and approved by Ali (an actual discount, the real free trial). Never invent one.

## 4. The content funnel: half for eyes, half for sales

- The brand's 11.7M-view reel (a game on a TV, funny, broad) sold less than a 46K-view reel (a hand opening a phone
  photo of the sweatpants, then the sweatpants themselves). Even with 11.7M fewer views, the small one converted far
  more.
- That is a content funnel: **half the reels get attention** (broad, entertaining, made to be shared) **so the other
  half can convert it** (the product, its detail, proof, the offer). Shown on screen as two clips with a red arrow:
  "For eyes" > "For sales".
- When planning a week or month for a client or for viZion, label every reel as *eyes* or *sales* and keep roughly
  half and half. Judge eyes reels on reach and follows, sales reels on DMs and orders, never one by the other's metric.
- When a reel goes viral, **double down**: post more in the same format while it is working. More posts, more chances.

## 5. The breakdown edit (talking head + proof above the head)

This is how all four breakdown reels are cut. Use it when Ali films himself (or a client films) explaining something.

- **Frame**: 1080x1920, the speaker at a desk or against a wall, framed mid-chest up, eyes about 40 % down the frame,
  phone lav mic in hand (it reads as honest, not studio). Real room, real light. The upper third above the head is
  kept free for proof.
- **Proof floats above the head**: the real reel being discussed, played as a 9:16 clip about 440 px wide, top edge
  near y 80, sitting in the space over the speaker's head; two clips side by side when comparing ("this one, or this
  one?"); up to three thumbnails with their view counts for a quick "these all went viral". When a clip is the point
  of the moment it takes the full screen (filmed off a phone or TV and pushed in until it fills the frame).
- **Real numbers as proof**: cropped screenshots of the real Insights (Views, Accounts reached, Average watch time,
  Follows, the retention graph, the views curve, likes and comment counts, the DM inbox scrolling, the profile header).
  They appear the moment the number is said. Only real screenshots from the real account: never rebuild them as fake
  UI and never invent a number (CLAUDE.md honesty and -noAI rules).
- **Reaction images** sit in the same slot for a beat of humour (a meme still, "Absolute cinema", an emoji next to a
  thumbnail). Use sparingly and only licensed or the user's own.
- **Everything enters on a hard cut**, on the word. No slides, pops, fades or blur on the overlays. The editing energy
  comes from the speed of the cuts and from the speaker's hands, not from animation.
- **Captions**: one word at a time, white, bold, a soft dark shadow, centred around y 1250 to 1300 (chest height,
  below the face, above the hands). Each word cuts in exactly on the voice; numbers are written as numbers
  ("30,000", "11.7 million", "100k?"). A short key line can stay up as one phrase ("Great hook = question"). In Arabic
  use the page's own font (DG Sahabah for @allawi.psd), word by word, never a blur reveal.
- **Watermark**: a small Instagram glyph with the handle in small caps under it, moved to a different place every few
  seconds (top right, middle left, lower left) so the reel cannot be cropped and reposted cleanly.
- **End card**: the last 2 seconds the whole frame darkens to about 30 %, the profile header screenshot stays at the
  top, and in the middle the Instagram glyph (white, cycling into the IG gradient and back) with the handle under it.
  Last spoken line is the follow ask with a wink: "follow the greatest marketer in the world", "hopefully I converted
  you into a follower".
- **Tone and pace**: fast, no pauses, no intro. The first words are the hook and they come with the proof on screen
  ("This two-second reel got me 30,000 followers and sold out a drop" + the reel + the Follows number).

## 6. A whole lesson as a joke

The first reel teaches the full method in "caveman" talk ("Viral video three part. Hook, build up, pay off.") with the
title "How to go viral for caveman." A plain lesson told in a funny voice is a shareable eyes reel on its own. In Iraqi
dialect this can be a deliberately simple, blunt version of a marketing idea.

## 7. Ending the music: three ways (sound reel)

Never stop the music dead. Pick one, using Ali's own sounds from `marketing/sfx-pack/`:

1. **The stem cut**: cut the melody stem out on the last beat and let only the reverb tail of the melody ring out
   (smooths the end).
2. **The reverb**: hard cut the whole beat at the end, and under the cut put a studio reverb tail of the music so it
   decays naturally.
3. **Riser + whoosh stack**: as the music ends, a riser/transition plus a mid whoosh and a low whoosh stacked on the
   same hit (e.g. `Clean Riser`, `whoosh clean`, `Whoosh Deep Short`).

The reel teaches it with the edit timeline on screen above the speaker's head: coloured track bars with handwritten
style labels and small arrows ("Music", "Melody", "Reverb, to smooth out the end"), numbered 1 to 3, each one played
as he names it. A real screen capture of the timeline, not a drawn diagram.

## 8. How to apply this for Ali

- **Pitches**: every reel pitch starts with the viewer's question and the second it is answered, says whether it is an
  *eyes* or a *sales* reel, and names the proof that will be on screen (which real reel, which real number).
- **Interactive reels**: offer at least one rewatch mechanic when pitching viZion or client reels (pause on the right
  frame, spot the bad logo, find the mistake in the ad), with the real reward hidden in the winning frame and a
  "screenshot to DM" call to action.
- **Breakdown reels**: if Ali can film himself, the strongest proof format for viZion and @allawi.psd is him breaking
  down his own work and results (a client ad, its real numbers, why it worked). Ask him to film it; edit it as in
  section 5. Never fill it with mock results.
- **Planning a month**: split it into eyes and sales reels about half and half, and when one format goes viral, make
  the next few in that format.
- **Reviewing a reel that flopped**: check, in this order, whether the first second raises a question, whether the
  build-up keeps it open, whether the payoff answers it with a feeling, and whether the reel was meant for eyes or
  for sales and judged by the right number.

## 9. Lesson from the fonts reel (29 Oct): never open on the answer

The «مطعم» fonts reel (12.8 s): a voice-over lesson on which font fits which food place, the words on a flat brown
field with tiny font names, a hidden 20 % discount frame (4 frames at 9.7 s, "screenshot it and DM"), then 3 s of a
dark end card. It reached 1,273 views, 97 % non-followers, from the Reels tab. People who stayed liked it (saves
1.6 % and shares 1.2 %, both above Ali's usual, 5 follows), but half had left by 0:02, average watch time was 4 s and
6 % reached the end. Why:
- The voice opened with an identity hook («عندك مطعم») but no pain point and no promise: it went straight into the
  value («للوجبات السريعة اختار خط عريض وواضح»). Reference 7 order: identity, pain, hook, then value.
- The first frame already showed the comparison (two fonts with their names), so there was no question to stay for.
  The answer came in the first 2 s, which is exactly the mistake reference 6 describes.
- The pause challenge was only a small orange line under the logo. The picture said "fonts lesson", not "game".
- The hidden frame came at 9.7 s, after about 90 % of viewers had gone, so almost nobody played.
- A lesson and a game in one reel fight each other: the lesson needs reading time, the game needs a short loop.
- The 3 s dark end card was a quarter of the reel and killed the replay. Zero comments: nobody was asked anything.

Before any reel ships, check it against Ali's retention references (6 and 7 in `references.md`):
- Frame 1 raises a question and does not show the answer. Show the problem first (a real sign or logo in the wrong
  font, a word whose font gets changed); hide the comparison or the verdict until the end.
- Hook in the first second, a delay that keeps the question open, a rehook around 0:04 to 0:06 (a new reveal or a
  twist where the graph usually drops), and then the answer.
- Cut the moment the payoff lands. No dead end card: the handle sits on the payoff frame, so the reel loops.
- Type a viewer can read on a phone in under a second. Small labels such as font names under the word count as noise.
- A design comparison is a comment game: "which one?", with the answer flashed on the last frame.
- A pause challenge is its own short reel (2 to 4 s): the challenge is the picture and the big headline, and the
  hidden frame sits early enough that most viewers pass it. Never bolt one onto the end of a lesson.
