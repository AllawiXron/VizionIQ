# Six everyday things that became logos

A 10-slide carousel for @allawi.psd (1080×1350, `out/post-1..10.png`), my answer to a reel that showed objects next to
logos without saying why they work. Each brand slide has the object cut from a real photo and lit on a field graded from
the brand's colour, the object's name set huge behind it, then the story and the one design detail that made a plain
object a brand.

1. Cover: a still life of the objects, guess the logo before swiping.
2. Apple (deep red): the real apple with the logo's bite cut out of it. Rob Janoff, 1977, added the bite so it is not
   taken for a cherry.
3. Lacoste (green): the crocodile head; the press nicknamed René Lacoste the crocodile after a bet over a crocodile-skin
   case, his friend Robert George drew it, side-on with the jaw open so it reads small on a shirt.
4. Shell (yellow): the founders' father sold seashells in London; the scallop you know was drawn by Raymond Loewy in 1971.
5. Red Bull (navy): two bronze bulls face to face with a sun between them, as the logo is built; taken from the Thai
   drink Krating Daeng.
6. Starbucks (green): an old two-tailed mermaid woodcut (1529, not Starbucks' own source, the slide says so); the siren
   came from a 16th century woodcut, and since 2011 the logo is the siren alone.
7. Mastercard: the two circles printed over a compass drawing, the overlap making the third colour; no name since 2019.
8. What they share: something simple people remember, plus one detail only you can claim; the six logos with theirs.
9. My كاكتوس logo, for a small project by a girl who crochets by hand: the real saguaro next to the knitted cactus,
   built from the logo's own layers (`../gift-cactus/layers/logo`).
10. Write your business in the comments, or DM.

- `v2/slides.html`: the slides; `node render.cjs` renders `out/` (with `NODE_PATH=/opt/node-tools/node_modules`).
- `v2/*.png`: the objects cut from their photos; `v2/knit-cactus.png` is the logo's cactus, pot and yarn layers.
- `caption.txt`: the caption, with the photo credits the licences ask for.
- `logos/`: the logo files from Wikimedia Commons and English Wikipedia; trademarks of their owners, shown to talk
  about them. `lacoste-croc.svg` is the crocodile cut from `lacoste.svg`.
