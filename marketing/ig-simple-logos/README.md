# Six everyday things that became logos

A 10-slide carousel for @allawi.psd (1080×1350, `out/logo-1..10.png`), my answer to a reel that showed objects next to
logos without saying why they work. Each slide here says what one detail made a plain object a brand, and slide 9 shows
how I used the same idea on the كاكتوس logo.

1. Cover: six photos of the objects, guess the logo before swiping.
2. Apple: the bite, added so nobody takes it for a cherry (Rob Janoff).
3. Lacoste: the press nicknamed René Lacoste the crocodile after a bet over a crocodile-skin case; on his shirts from 1933.
4. Shell: the founders' father sold seashells in London; the first logo was a mussel shell, then the scallop.
5. Red Bull: taken from the Thai drink Krating Daeng (red gaur); two bulls charging in front of a sun.
6. Starbucks: the two-tailed siren from an old 16th century woodcut; the company started in Seattle, a port city.
   The woodcut in the photo is another one, of the same kind (Leipzig, 1529), and the slide says so.
7. Mastercard: two circles since the sixties, shown without the name since 2019.
8. The idea: something simple people remember, plus one detail only you can claim.
9. My كاكتوس logo: a saguaro photo, the plain cactus shape from the logo's layers on tracing paper, and the finished
   knitted logo (`../gift-cactus`).
10. Write your business in the comments, or DM.

Each logo sits on a sheet of tracing paper laid over its photo.

- `slides.html`: the slides; `node render.cjs` renders `out/` (with `NODE_PATH=/opt/node-tools/node_modules`).
- `caption.txt`: the caption (photo and logo credits in its last line).
- `logos/`: the logo files from Wikimedia Commons and English Wikipedia; trademarks of their owners, shown to talk
  about them. `lacoste-croc.svg` is the crocodile cut from `lacoste.svg`.
- `photos/cactus-shape.png`: the body and spines layers of the كاكتوس logo.
